kalchas.live · mobile board · 390 × 844

A board that works on a phone

Three directions for phone widths (≤ 640px). Desktop stays exactly as it is: each option is a mobile-only component behind a media query. Rows are the live board at 16:28 today. Crests are stand-ins.

What breaks today at 390px

  • The first match sits 632px down. The hero (597px) and Recent signals (243px) fill the first screen.
  • The table is 1604px wide in a 369px window. You see star, League, Score and Match. Time, odds, K·idx, all five signals and stats are off-screen.
  • Swipe right and Match scrolls away. The numbers have no team next to them.
  • Six filter checkboxes wrap over three rows. Signal explanations live in hover tips, which a phone can't open.
  • The floating “here now” tile sits on top of the team names.
  • The match overlay works full-screen, with tabs and bars. All three options reuse it for the deep view.

InPlayGuru at 390px (public /inplay)

  • Still a table on phones. Their guide even calls the scanner “optimised for desktop”. What feels right is the chrome around it.
  • Time-window switch (Full / 5 / 10 / 15 / 20 min) and saved Layouts change which numbers you see, so the screen never has to show everything.
  • Full-screen Filters sheet with a “match funnel” (272 live, 272 make it through) and a sticky “Show 272 matches” button.
  • Bottom tab bar with a live-count badge. Controls fold into a 3 × 2 button grid instead of inline checkboxes.
  • Home and away stacked in one cell, plus a momentum sparkline per row.
  • Weak spot: no frozen Match column, same as ours. Logged out, tapping a row only highlights it.

Shared by all three

  • A 45px sticky top bar replaces the hero: mark, LIVE, people here, and a cream Join pill. The full pitch moves into the Join tab.
  • Filters move into a sheet. The six checkboxes keep their current defaults.
  • The pinned brass shelf carries over as-is.
  • Tapping a match opens the existing match overlay (Stats / Timeline / H2H / Lineups).
  • A tap opens what hover opens on desktop, so signal explanations are reachable.
A

Lens table

recommended

Still the board, without the sideways scroll. The match column is frozen, about 160px wide. A three-way lens picks what fills the rest: Signals, Stats or Odds. Every lens fits in 390px. Tap a header to sort. About 12 matches per screen on a 390 × 844 phone.

Tap the lens buttons in the first phone to switch.

Borrows from InPlayGuru

  • The time-window switch and Layouts idea: one control that changes which columns show.
  • Home over away stacked in each cell.
  • The filters sheet with a live funnel count and a sticky “Show N matches” button.
  • Bottom tab bar with count badges.

Fixes what they don't

  • The match column never scrolls away.

Costs

  • K·idx and the signals share one screen, but stats sit one tap away.
  • Team names clip around 15 characters.

Why this one

The board is for comparing one signal down a column and sorting by it. A keeps that. B and C give it up for nicer single matches.

B

Signal cards

option

One card per match. The header shows league, minute, pin and a gold K·idx badge. Teams sit with a large Fraunces score, then a five-tile strip in the signal tones. The footer carries SOT, DA, corners and TSLG. Tap a card to open bars and odds inline. About 3 matches per screen.

Borrows from InPlayGuru

  • Home and away read together inside each tile.
  • Filters reduced to compact one-tap controls, here as a chip row.
  • Recent signals as a swipeable strip, in the spirit of their alert toasts.

Costs

  • You can't run your eye down Δ5′ across 20 matches. Sorting still works, but it reads as a feed.
  • About four times the scrolling of A.
C

Pulse feed + sheet

option

The most glanceable. One slim row per match: K·idx large on the left, teams and score, then the minute and the single hottest signal. It turns orange when it fires. Tap a row and a bottom sheet opens with every signal, its home and away bars, and the hover-tip explanation in plain sight. About 12 rows per screen, the same as A, but each shows one signal instead of five.

Borrows from InPlayGuru

  • Tabs (Live / Signals / Pinned) with counts, like their In-Play badge.
  • “Phones are for alerts”: Recent signals gets its own tab instead of a panel.
  • One headline metric per row, the way their Momentum leads every row.

Costs

  • Four of the five signals hide behind a tap.
  • “Hottest” is a new rule we would have to define: the highest share of its own scale, with a fire beating everything.
